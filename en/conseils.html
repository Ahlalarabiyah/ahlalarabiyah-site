🎒 Equipment tips

Required equipment

  • Have at least two pens: a regular pen to write the dictation, and a red pen for corrections, vowels or important notes.
  • Have two large-squared notebooks: one for lessons and one for exercises.
  • Have headphones to hear the audio clearly and to better tell sounds and vowels apart.
  • Have a pencil and an eraser to practise, correct easily and write without fear of making a mistake.

Useful but optional equipment

  • A ruler to underline the words to correct or to organize your work.
  • Small cards to write down difficult words and review them.
  • A slate or whiteboard to practise writing letters and words.

📝 Dictation tips

General tips for dictations

Before you start

To succeed at a dictation with no mistakes, or very few, begin by reviewing the vocabulary you have already learned. The dictated words generally come from what you have already studied; there may occasionally be one or two new words: in that case, listen to them carefully.

During the first listen

On the first listen, don't try to write yet. Simply listen to discover what is going to be dictated and to prepare your mind.

If possible, try to understand the meaning of the sentences. Understanding what you hear will help you write the words with the correct spelling.

Then repeat aloud what you hear. This strengthens your auditory memory and helps you pronounce the words better.

While writing

Pick up your pen ✍🏾 and write during the second listen, especially if you know the words and understand the meaning of the sentences.

Tips to raise the difficulty: if you feel comfortable and confident, try to write the dictation from the second listen. For an even bigger challenge, you can even try to write it from the first listen. Succeeding at this challenge will prove that you have mastered dictation writing.

If you don't understand yet, or don't understand at all because you don't know the meaning of the words, listen a third time, then a fourth if necessary. As soon as you feel ready, pick up your pen and write.

While you write, repeat aloud what is being dictated. If possible, pause the audio player at the end of each sentence so you can organize your writing, then resume listening.

At the end of the dictation

At the end, reread what you wrote while listening to the audio again, without looking at the correction. Check the letters, the words and the sentences.

Correcting mistakes

Look at the correction and, if some words are misspelled, for example with the wrong letters, take your notebook and rewrite those words several times, even over several lines.

Say each word aloud while you write it. This will help you memorize it both visually and by ear.

My general method

⚠️ In general, I always do my homework by speaking aloud: I never keep anything only in my head. I repeat, I read aloud and I write.

💡 Tips for studying well

Organizing your learning

  • Review your module thoroughly before moving on to the next one.
  • Regularly review the lessons you have already learned, even when you move on to a new lesson.
  • Work a little every day rather than a lot all at once. Even ten to fifteen minutes, added up at different times of the day, can make a big difference.
  • Make use of commutes or other free moments to review, listen to the audio and move forward in your learning.
  • Take breaks when you feel tired. A rested mind learns better.

Improving at dictations

  • For dictations, follow the general tips: listen, repeat, write, then reread.
  • Move forward step by step: start with words, then short sentences, then longer sentences.
  • Reread your earlier dictations and correct the difficult words.
  • Repeat words and sentences aloud: it helps a lot with memorization and pronunciation.

Using the app

  • Play the games several times: the questions and words change on each repetition, which helps you review better.
  • Challenge a friend or relative you have referred: it can encourage you to progress together.

Staying confident

  • Don't compare your progress with others'. Everyone learns at their own pace.
  • Making mistakes is not a problem: they help you discover what you need to review.
  • Congratulate yourself for every bit of progress, however small.
